Amber on teal

Identical to the shipped Y2 — 4px band, 3.5px amber, 7.5px total. Only the dark stripe changes. Both site teals are here, because the two behave quite differently.

The joint is what moves

In a two-stripe mark the amber only ever gets one hard edge — the one facing the dark stripe. Its other side always dissolves into the white card. So the whole design rests on that single joint, and swapping navy for teal is really a question about that one boundary.

Navy gave it 10.32×. Button teal gives it 2.64×, which is close to the 1.65× that made the original amber invisible — so with #2D8587 the amber ends up with two soft edges instead of one, and the mark reads as a single blurred stripe rather than two. Deep teal recovers most of it at 4.18×.

Edge profiles at ×8. Watch the seam between the two colours, not the colours themselves.

One more thing worth weighing on Z1. #2D8587 is the View Roles button. Putting it on the band means the loudest colour on a picked card appears twice, and the card’s longest-standing rule — the CTA is the only saturated element — stops holding. Z2 avoids that by using the hover shade instead.

Z1

Button Teal

The exact teal from View Roles, #2D8587. Warmer and friendlier than navy, and it ties the mark to the brand colour rather than to a neutral. Two things to look at before you like it. The joint between the stripes falls to 2.64× — against navy it was 10.32× — so the amber’s one hard edge softens and the two stripes start to blur into a single warm-green smudge at arm’s length. And the band is now the same colour as the button four inches to its right, which is the one thing the card was built to avoid: the CTA stops being the only saturated element on the card.

Deep Teal

The darker --teal-dk, #226365 — the hover state of the button rather than its rest state. This is the teal that actually works: the joint recovers to 4.18× and the band sits at 6.46× against the page, so both stripes stay separate and the mark still reads as teal. It is also distinct enough from the button’s rest colour that the two do not look like the same component.

Where this lands

If you want teal, Z2. It keeps the two stripes legible and stays clear of the button. Z1 is the colour you asked for and it is genuinely warmer, but it costs the seam and doubles the brand colour on the card.

Honestly: neither beats the shipped navy on the mechanics. Navy is the only colour on the palette that gives the amber a 10× joint and a 15.95× band, and that is why Y2 landed. Teal is a taste call, not an improvement — which is a perfectly good reason to make it, as long as it is Z2.
