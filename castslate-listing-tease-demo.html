Hold something back for the click

Right now the card gives away the tagline and 200 characters of synopsis, so opening a casting shows an actor what they already read. Below: the same two real listings with the card teasing and the opened casting doing the revealing. The opened view at the bottom never changes — only what the card shows.

1 · Today. Full tagline plus roughly 200 characters of synopsis. On “The Back Tanks” the generator's tagline is 96 characters and repeats the synopsis almost word for word, so the card says the same thing twice and the detail page says it a third time.
2 · One line, faded — my pick. Tagline capped at 8 words, synopsis clamped to a single line that fades out mid-sentence, then “Open to read the full breakdown”. The fade is the important part: a hard ellipsis reads like a bug, a fade reads like there is more behind it. An actor gets the hook, the money and the deadline on the card — enough to decide to click, not enough to decide not to.
3 · Tagline only. No synopsis on the card at all; the tagline carries the whole hook. The purest version and the fastest to scan — a page of these reads like a headline list. The risk is real: if a tagline is weak or generic, the listing has nothing left to sell with, and you have 444 taglines of varying quality.
4 · Two lines + counter. A middle setting: two clamped lines plus an explicit “+ 2 more paragraphs, 8 roles inside”. Naming what is behind the click is the strongest pull of the four, but it also makes short listings look thin — a casting with one paragraph advertises that it has one paragraph.

To make this hold, the limit has to be enforced where the text is written, not only where it is displayed:
· CD post form — tagline field capped at 8 words / 60 characters with a live counter, and helper text that says what it is for: “One line that makes someone want to open this. Not a summary.”
· Casting generator — its taglines currently run up to 96 characters and often restate the synopsis verbatim (see “The Back Tanks” on tab 1). It should write a short hook line instead, drawn from the premise but never equal to it.
· Admin edit modal — same counter, so an admin editing a listing sees the same rule.
· Card display — clamp by lines, not characters, so the cut always lands on a clean edge whatever the length.
One caution: search and SEO read the synopsis. Clamping it visually with CSS keeps the full text in the page for crawlers — truncating the string in the database would not. Clamp, do not cut.
