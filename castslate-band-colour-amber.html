Bright is not the same as visible

All three are the shipped banded edge, thinned from 8px to 6px, with only the two colours changed. Amber is in all of them — the question is whether it carries the band or the hairline.

On #FFBF00, honestly

You asked for it, so it is Demo T and I have built it to its best. But it has a real problem worth knowing before you choose: pure amber is almost exactly as light as the cream page it sits on. Against #FAF7F1 it measures 1.55×. The shipped brass measures 4.95× — more than three times the separation.

Saturation and lightness are different things, and only one of them helps you find a card. Amber looks vivid on a swatch because its chroma is at maximum, but a band is found by its value — how dark it is against the paper behind it. Two colours can be equally light and wildly different in hue, and when they are, the eye scanning a long page does not stop.

This is not an argument against amber. It is an argument about which part of the mark amber should be. Demo U keeps your exact colour and moves it to the hairline, where a 1.5px line of pure amber against navy is the brightest thing on the card — and the band behind it does the finding.

T

Marquee

Your colour, built as strongly as it can be. Pure amber at 6px, anchored by a deep-brass hairline instead of a light one — a bright band with a light line beside it has nothing to sit against and floats off the paper. This is amber at its best on this card. It is also, at 1.55× against the page, the least findable band of the three by a wide margin. Scroll the page and judge it in motion rather than in close-up, because that is the gap between how it looks stopped and how it works in a list.

Amber on Ink

The same two colours, swapped.
