Why the reel is the weak one. It's accurate — it is a film object — but it's generic. It says "film," and so does the clapperboard next to it and the strip next to that. It carries no information about what makes an independent film different, so it's the tile an actor learns nothing from.

1 · Festival laurel — my pick. Laurels are the universal shorthand for independent film; every indie poster carries them, and actors know them from Sundance, SXSW and every festival selection card. It's the only shape in the family that isn't a piece of equipment, so it reads instantly at a glance in a list. It also stops the family from being five variations on "camera gear."

2 · Laurel + frame spells it out — festival plus film — but at 28px the frame inside the wreath gets tight. Use this only if the laurel alone feels too abstract to you.

3 · Director's chair is distinctive and reads well small, but "director" isn't specifically independent — a studio feature has a director's chair too.

4 · Camera on tripod suggests small-crew production, which is right, but it sits close to Documentary's camcorder and the two could blur in a scroll.
