Working prototype

Super Assistant, your acting coach.

An actor career assistant that reads like a text thread — and teaches a full acting course inside the thread. Super Assistant knows CastSlate cold and knows the business around it, but the point is that it does not need a pre-written lesson to teach you something. Ask it how to stop moving your hands, how to play a liar, what a spike mark is, why casting skipped your reel after four seconds, or why every take you do looks the same, and it builds the lesson.

Who is texting

Super Assistant greets and closes differently for each. A Premium member is never sold to — it shows them how to use what they already pay for.

Experience level

Lessons adapt. Beginners get plain language and one exercise at a time; experienced actors get the extra layer — lens size, coverage, contract shape, competing techniques.

How it is wired

    
    
      
  • Three layers, checked in order. Small talk and identity first, then a refusal filter, then retrieval over the knowledge base. That is why "hey" gets a greeting instead of a help article.
    • 
      
  • Plan awareness. The caller passes plan and first name. A free actor at the weekly cap gets the upgrade path; a Premium member gets a walkthrough and no sales card, ever.
    • 
      
  • It teaches, not just answers. 23 polished flagship lessons run as multi-step exchanges — concept, why it matters, an exercise you do on the spot, what to look for, a challenge, a takeaway. A visitor is offered a free one before accounts are ever mentioned, and is told outright that the whole curriculum is free and they only need a notepad.
    • 
      
  • The lesson library is a toolkit, not a boundary. A concept graph of 62 acting concepts stores teaching components — the idea, the reason, an example, an exercise, what to observe, the common mistake, the correction, a challenge, a takeaway. A composer assembles those into a quick tip, a standard lesson, a deep dive or a masterclass at answer time. "How do I stop moving my hands?" has no written lesson and still gets a real one.
    • 
      
  • Question-shape routing. Habit / state / diagnostic / craft / vocabulary questions get different teaching frames. A legitimate actor question with no matching concept still gets taught — from principles, with the method made explicit rather than faked.
    • 
      
  • Interactive coaching. Say "it still didn't work" mid-lesson and she corrects rather than advancing: don't try to remove it — trying to remove something is still attention on yourself.
    • 
      
  • Learner memory. "I always overact" or "I'm fine on stage but stiff on camera" is captured from ordinary conversation and biases which exercises she picks and what she offers next.
    • 
      
  • 62-term set, stage and industry glossary — hold, first refusal, pinned, under-five, upgrade, room tone, martini, MOS — each with the definition plus the thing nobody tells you.
    • 
      
  • Three knowledge layers. CastSlate product facts (from the site's own constants), general entertainment-industry knowledge, and anything time-sensitive — SAG-AFTRA rates, current contract terms, agency policies — which she explains structurally and sends you to the authoritative source for rather than quoting numbers that go stale.
    • 
      
  • Answers the question before it mentions CastSlate. An industry answer is an industry answer. The platform comes in where it genuinely solves the problem being discussed — the QR promo card in the agent question, Manager Mode in the what-do-I-do-this-week question — and not otherwise.
    • 
      
  • Never compares platforms. Any rival named gets one honest line — it cannot compare — then what CastSlate does, stated as fact.
    • 
      
  • Honest selling. Price, renewal price and the free path in the same breath. Never a claim that Premium gets anyone cast.
    • 
      
  • Escalation. Billing disputes, deletion, safety reports, anything about a minor, or two consecutive misses hand off to the human team.
