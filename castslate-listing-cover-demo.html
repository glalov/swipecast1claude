Listing covers — five treatments

Same four real listings from your live board, same card, same scan strip in every tab. Only the cover treatment changes, so you are comparing one variable. Nothing here is a photograph.

1 · No cover, strip only. The cheapest option and still a real improvement over today: the deciding facts sit above the synopsis instead of below it. If you ship nothing else, ship this.
2 · Colour — the version you flagged. Six saturated duotones down a ten-listing page reads as a rainbow ladder, and the hues collide with colours that already carry meaning in your UI: teal is live, purple is Cast Slate Pick, red is closing. Decorative colour borrowing those signals is the real problem, not just the loudness.
3 · Ink — my revised pick. One material, no hue. Variation comes from tone, light direction, angle and type size, so no two covers match while the board stays quiet. This is the treatment that sits inside your cream-and-ink palette instead of arguing with it, and it never competes with a badge for attention.
4 · Paper. The inverse: a warm paper card with ink type, like a printed call sheet. Softest and most editorial of the set. Risk to weigh — it is close in value to the white card it sits on, so it needs its hairline border to stay a distinct object, and on a long page it can read as slightly flat.
5 · Category tint. Colour returns, but only as information: one deeply muted hue per project type — film slate-blue, theatre plum, student moss, commercial umber. An actor learns the code in a day and can filter the page with their eyes. More useful than 3, slightly louder; the honest tradeoff is that it only pays off if you keep the type list short and stable.
6 · Slate. One device for the entire board — the clapper stripe, which is literally your name. Strongest brand read of the five and the most "poster wall" of them. Weakness: every cover shares one silhouette, so distinctiveness rests entirely on the title type.
