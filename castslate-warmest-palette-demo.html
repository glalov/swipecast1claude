The two that matter. --t1 is the whole thing — a cool navy at hue 240 was the last large cold surface on a warm page, and it's on every title. And --s1: pure white cards on cream read as cold cut-outs pasted onto the page; a warm white makes the card belong to the paper.

What I will not warm, and this is the commitment you asked for: the teal. #2D8587 is cool at hue 181, and warming it would technically make the page warmer — but it's your brand: the button, the logo lockup, the links, the emails. Warmth bought by dissolving your identity is a bad trade, and a page where everything is one temperature has nothing to look at. The teal is now the only cool thing left, which is exactly what makes the CTA pull your eye.

The navy stays alive as a brand colour in the logo and dark sections — this changes reading text only. --t2 and --t3 are already warm from this afternoon, so they don't move. --t1 gets slightly darker against a slightly lighter ground, so contrast improves rather than degrades.
