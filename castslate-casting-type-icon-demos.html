CastSlate · browse card

Casting type icons — three visual languages

Three complete icon systems for the browse card, each with its own drawing style and its own position on the card. Same nine families in all three, so you are comparing the look and the placement, not the coverage.

Why nine families and not one icon per type. The castings table currently holds 85 distinct type strings — “Short Film”, “Proof of Concept”, “Off-Off-Broadway Theater”, “Horror Short Talent Pool”, “Hand Model”, “VR / AR Project”. You cannot draw 85 icons, and you should not: an icon that appears once is decoration, not a signal. Every set below normalises those 85 strings into 9 families, so each mark is seen often enough to be learned. The long tail of “… Talent Pool” and “… Roster” types is big enough that it earns its own family rather than being dumped into “Other”.

Demo 1

Slate Etch

Hairline monoline glyphs — 1.5px stroke, open geometry, nothing filled. Drawn to feel scratched into a slate rather than dropped in from an icon set. The mark lives inside the type chip at the top-left, so it costs zero extra height and simply gives the existing badge a face.

Position — inline, leading the type chip · 17px · replaces .cc-badge

Demo 2

Gel Chip

The opposite approach — solid silhouettes, no strokes, sitting on a soft tinted tile with its own colour per family. Detail is knocked out of the shape in the tile’s own tint. Because each family owns a hue, a page of ten cards becomes scannable by colour before you read a single word. The tile gets its own column on the left of the card.

Position — dedicated 52px left column, top-aligned with the title

Demo 3

Marquee Stencil

The glyph is cut out of a solid navy block — a real hole, not a drawn line — and the block carries two semicircular notches like a torn theatre ticket. The most distinctive silhouette of the three, so it goes to the top-right corner, overhanging the card edge with a cream halo so it reads as a stamp pressed onto the listing rather than a second control sitting beside the button.

Position — corner stamp overhanging the top-right edge, 40px
