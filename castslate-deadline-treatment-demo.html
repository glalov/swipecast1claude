No clock on the wall

An actor who reads “14 days left to apply” learns nothing useful and one bad thing: that a clock is running out on them. Craigslist shows no expiry at all — only how long ago a post went up — and it works. Two changes here, and they answer the same fear from opposite ends: while a casting is open, nothing on it mentions time except how fresh it is — and the scroll now says out loud where the live castings end, so nine pages of closed listings read as a catalogue instead of as chances the actor missed.

The formula

Four rules. Three of them are about one casting; the last one is about the scroll they all sit in.
  1. Open — day 1 to the last day. Nothing about the deadline anywhere. No countdown chip on the card, no DEADLINE row on the page, no “closes Sep 17” under the Apply button. The only time signal is “Posted two weeks ago”, and beside it “Casting now — apply today” with the tooltip you already wrote: an older post does not mean the role is taken.
  2. The final day. One quiet line — “Last day to apply” — and nowhere else. It reads as an invitation, not as a clock: nobody is told they are late, they are told today counts. This is the only place a date is implied at all, and it is optional; say the word and it goes too.
  3. Closed. Unambiguous and calm: “Applications closed”, the Apply panel replaced rather than left dangling — and immediately under it, the open castings that are still taking submissions. A closed listing must never be a dead end; that is where hope is actually lost, not in a date.
  4. The list itself. Browse already sinks closed castings below every live one, so the real scroll is about two and a half pages of open work followed by nine pages of finished work — with nothing marking the border. One line marks it: “You have seen every casting taking submissions right now — all 26 of them.” Same cards, same order, nothing hidden. It turns an endless pile of missed chances into a finite set an actor can actually finish, and an archive that proves the place is busy.

Why not keep a small countdown

“13 days left” is read as “12 days from now this is worthless.” It gives permission to come back later, and later is usually never — the same reason the browse card already hides any countdown over 14 days.

Why the deadline row goes too

A date in a facts table is still a clock. An actor comparing two castings will pick the one with more days left, which has nothing to do with whether they are right for the part.

What the CD still sees

The deadline stays exactly where it belongs — on the casting form, in the CD's own dashboard, and in the admin row. It keeps driving when the listing closes. It just stops being shouted at the actor.
