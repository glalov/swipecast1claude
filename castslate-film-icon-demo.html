Why the current one is the weak link. Your other tiles are all literal objects — a megaphone for Commercial, a monitor with a play button for Web Series, a rocket for Pitch Trailer, an open spread for Print. Film is three abstract shapes. Next to literal neighbours, abstraction doesn't read as "modern," it reads as "unfinished."

Film strip — my pick. It's the only one of these that no one can misread, it holds together at 28px because the perforations are chunky rather than fine, and its horizontal rectangle is a different silhouette from the Web Series monitor, so the two don't get confused when they sit near each other in a list.

Film reel is equally recognisable but it's a circle, and at 28px a circle with holes can flicker between "reel" and "gear." It also fights the round pill shapes elsewhere on the card.

Movie camera is the most characterful and the most fun. It carries the most detail, so it's the first to get mushy on a low-DPI screen. If you like personality over legibility, this is the one.

Clapperboard is the strongest film signal that exists — and that's the problem. It's your logo. On a card it would read as a CastSlate badge rather than a project type, especially next to your "Cast Slate Pick" label.
