What changed and why — this is my actual reasoning, not decoration.

  • Serif headline. Geometric sans at weight 800 with tight tracking reads as urgent and commercial — it's the voice of a deals site. A serif at weight 600 reads as editorial and considered: someone wrote this, a person is behind it. For a casting platform, where the fear is "is this real?", the typeface is doing credibility work as much as warmth work. Your emails already use a serif headline, so this makes the site and the inbox sound like the same company.
  • Weight 800 → 600. Heavy type is loud. Loud is not welcoming. This is the single biggest change on the card.
  • Line-height 1.45 → 1.64 on body, 1.15 → 1.24 on the title. Air between lines is the most reliable "this is calm" signal there is, and it costs nothing but pixels.
  • Card padding 20 → 26px, and real space before the chip strip. Crowding reads as pressure. Space reads as confidence.
  • Chip radius 8 → 10, weight 700 → 600. Softer corners, less shouting.
  • Ink #1A1A2E → #23201C on card text. A warm near-black instead of a cool navy. I'd keep the navy as the brand colour in the logo and buttons — this only changes reading text.
  • Measure capped at ~62 characters. Long lines are tiring; a comfortable measure is one of the few typographic rules with genuinely solid evidence behind it.

What I deliberately did not change: the palette, the greens, the teal button, the badges. We fixed those already, and stacking more change would make it impossible to tell what worked.

My honest confidence: high on weight, line-height and spacing — those are close to settled craft. Medium on the serif, which is a voice decision: it will make the site feel more like a publication and less like a marketplace. If you want the marketplace feel, keep DM Sans and take everything else. That's the one line in here I'd call taste rather than craft, and I'll say so rather than dress it up.
