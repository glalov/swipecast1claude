Rules that don’t begin

You called the last three stiff and broken, and broken is the exact right word. Every one of them started and stopped dead in the middle of an edge. That is the thing that was wrong — not the weight, not the colour.

A line with two hard ends can only ever look stuck on

Look at where the earlier rules failed and it is always the same two pixels: the top end and the bottom end. A bar that stops flat halfway down a card edge announces that someone put it there. The bracket was worse, because it had four hard ends. None of it was the weight or the colour — a rule can be beautiful at any weight if you cannot find where it begins.

So all three below dissolve at their ends instead of stopping. And one thing needs saying plainly, because it looks like a step backwards: these use a gradient, and it is not the gradient that sank the first attempt. That one ran across a rule’s width to fake a cylinder catching light — simulated shine, which is what read as plated plastic. These run along the length and only remove coverage; the colour itself stays a single flat value the whole way down. One is a fake highlight. The other is how an engraved rule behaves where the plate lifts off the paper.

Unchanged: no motion of any kind, the teal View Roles button is still the only saturated element, and no rule is thicker than 2px.

Taper

The same single rule, with its ends taken away. It rises out of nothing near the top of the card and falls back into nothing near the bottom, so there is no point you can put a finger on and say it starts here. That is the whole change, and it is the one that stops a rule looking applied — hard terminals are what make a line read as something laid onto a card rather than something the card has.

The Corner

This one adds no new shape at all. It picks up the radius the card already has and traces it — a little way along the top, a little way down the side — then fades out on both runs. Because it is drawn from the card’s own geometry rather than placed against it, there is nothing to notice as an addition. It is the closest thing here to special without trying: it looks like the card was always cornered and you only just saw it.

Before you pick the gilt one

P is adjacent to something already rejected, and you should know that rather than find out later. A warm wash across the card was carried through three revisions and dropped, because content sits on a wash and every casting exposes a different amount of it — two picks never looked equal, and the card that appeared warmer was measured showing less background, not more.

P is not that, and the reason is specific: the warmth stops thirty pixels from the edge, and no chip, badge or pill ever reaches it. Nothing sits on it, so nothing can vary. But it is the same family of idea, and it is the one to look at hardest on a real page of ten listings rather than three.
