Type scale and colour — four treatments

Your four real listings, same layout, no covers. Only size, weight and colour change between tabs. Backstage runs roughly a 27px title, 16px body and 14–15px meta; yours currently runs 22 / 13 / 12 / 11, which is the gap you noticed.

1 · What you have now. Title 22px, synopsis 13px, facts 12px, badges 11px. Everything is legible up close, but the facts an actor decides on are the smallest and palest text on the card — the hierarchy is upside down.
2 · Backstage scale, your palette. Title 27px, synopsis 16px, chips 14px, button 15px, and the body text darkened from #5b5f68 to #33373f. Nothing else changed. This alone is most of the difference you were reacting to — it reads at arm's length instead of leaning in.
3 · Scale plus colour weighting — my pick. Same sizes as 2, but the facts stop being decorative grey: pay is a solid green block (the single most-scanned number on a casting), closing date is warm red, freshness is violet, roles is your blue. The type/union badges are dialled down to neutral grey so they stop competing with the things that actually decide a submission. Backstage does exactly this — one loud accent for the action, muted furniture around it.
4 · Editorial. Title up to 31px, synopsis 17px, more padding, and the chips kept quiet. The most premium and the most magazine-like of the four; the trade is that fewer listings fit on a screen, which matters when someone is scanning 108 of them.
