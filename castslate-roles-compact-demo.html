Roles on the card — three compact layouts

Same information as the version you liked, but not a right-hand rail and much shorter. Each layout still shows three roles with age, gender and rate, and still says how many are left. Real roles and real rates from your database.

1 · Stacked rows. The version from the last demo, here only as the height benchmark. Three roles, each on its own line with its own button — roughly 168px of card. Clear, but it is what makes the card too tall.
2 · Role pills — my pick. Each role becomes one pill: part, then age and gender, then the rate in a green capsule. They wrap like tags, so three roles cost about 40px instead of 168. Nothing is on a rail, nothing is stacked, and the whole row still reads left to right in one pass. Tapping a pill opens the casting on that role.
3 · Three tiles. A three-column strip: part on top, specs under it, rate below, with a dashed fourth tile for the remainder. About 78px. More structured and more "spec sheet" than the pills; it also holds its shape better when one role has a long label.
4 · One-line roster. The roles written as a single sentence of specs — Lead F 22–32 · Lead M 24–35 · Supporting F 30–45 · +5 more. About 24px, the smallest possible way to say it. Densest and the most scannable at speed; the trade is that no individual role is clickable, so every actor lands on the same detail page.
