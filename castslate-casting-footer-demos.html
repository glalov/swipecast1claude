Casting page — the block below the roles

Six replacements for the current “Think you're right for multiple roles?” box, which today shows the same thing to everyone. Three for logged-out visitors, three for signed-in talent. Nothing here is live — these are drafts to pick from.

One deliberate departure from the Backstage example: Backstage's block is a gate — it withholds the audition details until you join. CastSlate already shows every role, rate and date to logged-out visitors, so a gate would be both a downgrade and untrue. These sell the submission instead: you've seen everything, you need a profile to act on it.

Goal: keep them moving, not sell to them

They can already submit, so a join pitch is noise. The useful jobs at the bottom of the page are: give them the next casting, tell them where they stand on this one, or make the submission they're about to send a better one.

Demo 4More Like ThisRecommended

The classic end-of-page move, and the right default: a casting page is a dead end otherwise. Matches on city and project type, excludes ones they've already submitted to. Directly lifts submissions per session, and for free users it quietly creates the weekly-cap pressure that sells Premium — without a single word of pitch.
