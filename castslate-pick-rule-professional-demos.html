The vertical rule, three professional cuts

All three keep Demo F’s idea — a mark on the left edge instead of a frame around the card — and fix the thing that made it read cheap. Each pick sits next to an ordinary card, because that is the only honest way to judge one.

What actually made Demo F look like a carnival. It was not the stripe, it was the gradient. Demo F used three stops — pale gold at the top, full gold in the middle, dark bronze at the bottom — which is the exact recipe for simulated shine. A surface that appears to catch light is reading as plated plastic, and plated plastic is prize-ribbon, raffle-booth, employee-of-the-month. Real brass and real foil in print are flat; they get their richness from being dark and low-chroma, not from glinting.

So every rule below is a single flat colour, and every colour has been pulled down out of the yellows into the browns. Left is the old gradient, right is what replaced it:

The second rule is containment. A stripe that runs off the top and bottom of a card looks stuck on; the same stripe clipped to the card’s corner radius, or inset and cut to a measured height, looks built in. Both moves are below.

Unchanged from the existing card, deliberately: the teal View Roles button stays the only saturated element, there is no wash on the paper, and no rule is thickened past the point where it stops reading as a rule.

Demo H

Ledger Rule

One flat 4px rule in deep brass, running the full height and clipped to the card’s corner radius, so it curves with the card instead of sitting on top of it. That clip is the entire difference between a printed rule and a sticker — it makes the mark a property of the card rather than something attached to it, which is exactly how the rejected clapper rail failed. Nothing else changes. Closest reference: a masthead rule in a trade daily.

Demo I

Brass Inlay

The rule pulls inside the card — 3px, inset 11px from the edge, square-ended, and cut to exactly the height of the project-type tile beside it. Nothing runs off an edge, so it reads as an inlaid strip set into a surface rather than a highlight passing through. The card border also warms by one step to tie the two together. Closest reference: brass inlay in a lobby wall.

Demo J

Playbill Spine

The rule widens to 26px and takes a job: the words Cast Slate Pick set vertically in the spine, foil-stamped cream on brass, reading top-to-bottom the way a book spine does on an American shelf. The body pill comes off, because the spine already says it. This is the only one of the three a reader could describe without pointing. Closest reference: a theatre programme, or a Criterion spine.
