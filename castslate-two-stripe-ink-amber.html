Ink and amber. Two.

The closing sliver did fix the amber, but it turned the mark into navy–amber–navy, and three stripes is a ribbon. These are two stripes only, and the amber is made visible the honest way — by giving it width.

The trade, stated plainly

With only two stripes, one of the amber’s edges will always be soft — that is a property of the colour, not a flaw in the design. Amber is 10.32× against navy and about 1.6× against both the white card and the cream page, so wherever the amber touches paper, that boundary is weak. Nothing except a third stripe can close both sides, and you have ruled that out, correctly.

So the only real lever left is width, and the only real choice is which side is soft. Y1 and Y2 put the soft edge on the inside against the card. Y3 swaps the order so the soft edge faces the page instead. All three are two stripes and nothing else.

Where this lands

Y2. Same 7.5px total you already approved, and at 3.5px the amber is wide enough that losing one edge costs it little — a 3.5px line with one hard edge still reads as a line, where a 1.5px one did not. It is also the only version that looks deliberately two-tone rather than like a navy band with something small next to it.

Y3 is worth a look before you decide. It is the boldest by feel, and if the amber meeting the cream page does not bother you on screen, it is the most amber the mark can be at only 3px.
