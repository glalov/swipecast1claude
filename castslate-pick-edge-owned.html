Marks that own the edge

You said vague, and vague is fair — I took the ends off the rule until there was almost nothing left to see. These three do the opposite. No fade, no taper, nothing dissolving: each one is the left side of the card.

I over-corrected, twice

Worth saying plainly, because the path here has been a pendulum and you have been the one absorbing it:

gradient bar → carnival flat hairlines → stiff tapered → vague this set → committed

Each fix answered the last complaint and overshot it. Taking the shine off made the marks correct and dead; dissolving the ends fixed dead and made them invisible. A mark that defines an edge has to be present the whole way down it — that is what an edge is. So nothing below fades, and nothing below is under 6px.

Colour is deliberately held constant at #8A6524 across all three so you are judging form only. It swaps to any of the three trial colours in one line, so treat hue as a separate dial and ignore it here.

Still unchanged: no motion, no gradient, and the teal View Roles button remains the only saturated element.

Q

Cut Edge

The card’s own outline changes. Its two left corners go square while the right stays rounded, and the flat side carries a solid 6px band. This is the only version where a pick is still identifiable with the colour removed entirely — take the brass out and the silhouette alone still tells you. That is as far from vague as this can go without shouting: nothing is added to the card, the card is simply cut differently.

That one extra line is the difference between the card looking coloured-in and the card looking bound.
