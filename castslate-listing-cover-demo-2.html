Round two — light, and nothing repeated

You were right twice. The old cover printed the title, type, role count and city — all four already sit in the row beside it. And a column of dark squares builds a black rail down a cream page. So: every cover here is light, and none of them repeats a word the row already says. Scan strip on in all tabs.

1 · No cover. Strip only, for reference. Zero redundancy by definition, zero weight, and no visual anchor — listings still blur together on a fast scroll.
2 · Monogram. A light sand tile carrying the title's initials in Anton and nothing else. It gives the eye a landmark and a listing an identity, without restating one word of the row. Warm, editorial, and it never darkens the page. My pick of this set.
3 · Drawn mark. No letters at all — generated geometry: arcs, rule lines, a halftone field, each seeded from the casting id. The most "designed" of the set and completely non-repetitive with the text. Weigh it honestly: abstract marks carry no meaning, so they decorate rather than inform.
4 · Quiet mark. The minimum that still reads as intentional — one ink square, one thin circle, sand ground. Nearly weightless on a long page. If 2 feels like too much personality, this is the same idea with the volume down.
5 · Edge only. No tile at all: a 5px tonal edge on the card, varying per listing. Costs nothing, adds rhythm, adds zero redundancy and zero weight — and it is the one option that changes nothing about your layout. If you want the smallest possible move that still makes the board feel considered, this is it.
